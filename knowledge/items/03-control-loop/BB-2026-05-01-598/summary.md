## Core Takeaway

This Guardian opinion piece by Bruce Schneier and Nathan E Sanders examines a landmark German court decision that declared Google liable for its AI overviews, rejecting defenses that users should not blindly trust AI. The authors trace the historical distinction between 'carriers' (like phone companies) and 'publishers' (like newspapers), and how internet companies have exploited Section 230's protections. They argue that AI summaries and chatbots function as publishers, exercising editorial discretion, and therefore companies should be fully responsible for their AI agents' outputs—just as they would be for human employees. The article cites the Air Canada chatbot case and the Ashley MacIsaac defamation lawsuit to illustrate real-world consequences. The authors conclude that allowing companies to hide behind faulty AI would create perverse incentives and erode trust, and they support holding AI systems to the same liability standards as human systems.
