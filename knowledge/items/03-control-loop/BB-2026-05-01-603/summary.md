## Core Takeaway

Based on extensive interviews with national leaders, defense ministers, intelligence chiefs, and diplomats across Eastern Europe, this article documents the 18-month erosion of trust in American security guarantees since Trump's second term. It traces key moments: Pete Hegseth's February 2025 speech declaring European security no longer a U.S. priority, the humiliating Oval Office showdown with Zelenskyy, the brief intelligence cutoff to Ukraine, the September 2025 Russian drone incursion into Polish airspace where Trump downplayed the attack, and the cancellation and reversal of U.S. troop rotations to Poland. The reporting captures a fundamental psychological shift: from viewing Trump's demands for higher European defense spending as a justified 'cold shower' to recognizing that U.S. commitment is now contingent on personal relationships and daily whims. European leaders are split between a strategy of flattery (personified by NATO chief Rutte) and quiet contingency planning. Smaller states like Latvia and Estonia report being locked out of decision-making circles. The piece concludes with the question of whether Europe should continue placating Trump or begin openly preparing for a future without the U.S.
