## Core Takeaway

Starting from the keynote of NVIDIA GTC, the article systematically outlines the core viewpoint proposed by Jensen Huang: computing hardware design is shifting from 'designed for human users' to 'designed for intelligent agents.' The author details four reasons why agents will become the next core of computing power (freeing productivity, reducing hidden resource consumption, ignoring quantity limits, and being more versatile than LLMs), and introduces the Vera Rubin enterprise platform designed for this purpose. The article focuses on NVIDIA's first consumer-grade CPU—the RTX Spark N1X—analyzing its ARM architecture, collaboration with MediaTek, 1 PFLOPS AI computing power, comprehensive technical adaptation for the Windows on ARM ecosystem (RTX, CUDA, DLSS, etc.), and partner products like the Microsoft Surface Laptop Ultra. At the same time, the article maintains a rational perspective, pointing out that the N1X is essentially based on the previous generation Grace Blackwell platform, not the latest architecture, and that the long-standing shortcomings of the Win on ARM ecosystem have not been resolved by NVIDIA's entry. The full text offers both forward-looking judgments on industry trends and technical breakdowns and risk warnings for specific products.
