## Core Takeaway

This article provides an in-depth interpretation of a survey paper on Agent Harness jointly published by institutions including CMU, Yale, and JHU. It points out that the current bottleneck in Agent research has shifted from model capabilities to the engineering system outside the model, namely the Agent Harness. The authors propose the ETCLOVG seven-layer framework, covering Execution Environment, Tool Interface, Context Management, Lifecycle Orchestration, Observability, Validation and Evaluation, and Safety and Governance. The article outlines three major engineering migrations from Prompt Engineering to Context Engineering to Harness Engineering, and emphasizes that observability and governance are independent core layers for production-grade Agents. It also discusses cross-layer issues such as the cost-quality-speed triangle and the capability-control paradox, noting that competition among Agent platforms will revolve around the complete set of Harness capabilities. Finally, the article reminds that Harness design should dynamically adjust as model capabilities evolve, avoiding over-scaffolding.
