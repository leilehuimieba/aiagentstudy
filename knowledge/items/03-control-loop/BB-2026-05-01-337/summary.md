## Core Takeaway

This article presents a methodology for AI collaboration derived deductively from fundamental facts, with the core idea of treating AI Agents as deep collaborators in software engineering. The author first establishes three axioms: information loss in the intention-to-code chain, the essential characteristics of LLMs (context determines output, probabilistic output, limited working memory), and human cognition as a scarce resource. It then examines five common assumptions—such as feeding code to AI enables project understanding, or AI is unsuitable for complex systems—and points out their fallacies. Based on this, the article proposes six best practices: Context Engineering (Spec-First, Docs as Code, progressive disclosure), human-machine division of labor (Johari Window model), full-chain AI participation (from requirements to coding), small tasks with multi-layer validation, Knowledge as Code (encoding knowledge into structured Skills), and an Error-Driven feedback loop. Together, these practices form a comprehensive methodology for AI-assisted software development.
