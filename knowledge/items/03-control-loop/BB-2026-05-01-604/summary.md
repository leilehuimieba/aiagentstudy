## Core Takeaway

Barney Ronay's column dissects the universally booed hydration breaks at the 2026 World Cup, exposing them as thinly veiled advertising intervals. He traces the negative fan reaction across nations, notes that only Ralf Rangnick and Gianni Infantino seem to support them, and argues that the breaks amount to a structural change – turning football into a four‑quarter game – comparable to anything since the codification of 45‑minute halves in 1897. Ronay dismisses the player‑welfare pretext as a Trojan horse for FIFA's revenue ambitions, especially in the US market, and shows how the breaks allow coaches like Ancelotti to manipulate momentum, undermining the game's essence. He concludes that football's beauty lies in its uninterrupted, often difficult rhythm, and urges fans to keep booing this 'act of vandalism'.
