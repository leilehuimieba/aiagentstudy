## Core Takeaway

This article provides a detailed interpretation of Microsoft's paper SkillOpt, which introduces a method for optimizing an Agent's Skill documents (i.e., prompts, manuals, or workflow specifications) as trainable external states. The article points out that traditional Skill documents are either written manually or generated by LLMs in a single pass, lacking a feedback loop. SkillOpt, in contrast, mimics the deep learning training paradigm by treating Skill documents as parameters and iteratively optimizing them in text space through mechanisms such as rollout (executing tasks), reflection (analyzing trajectories), bounded edits (constrained modifications), validation gates (verification), and rejected-edit buffers (negative feedback). Experimental results show that SkillOpt achieves optimal or tied-optimal performance across all 52 evaluation items, with significant improvements over baselines without Skills on multiple benchmarks, especially on tool-based tasks like Spreadsheet. The article also analyzes ablation study results for core components of SkillOpt (such as learning rate, rejected buffer, and slow update) and provides simplified recommendations for real-world implementation.
