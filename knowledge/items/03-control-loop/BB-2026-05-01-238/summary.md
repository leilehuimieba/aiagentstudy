## Core Takeaway

Using the example of a 3-7 person team at OpenAI generating nearly 1 million lines of production-grade code with AI in 5 months, the article systematically outlines the three evolutions of AI engineering. The first evolution, Prompt Engineering, addresses the issue of 'saying it clearly,' but its marginal benefits diminish as model comprehension improves. The second evolution, Context Engineering, tackles the problem of 'providing sufficient information,' encompassing key techniques such as RAG, context compression, and a single source of truth. The third evolution, Harness Engineering, solves the challenge of 'system reliability,' leveraging strategies like context governance, validation loops, and technical debt cleanup to manage AI. The article emphasizes that these three are nested layers, and introduces the Harness Decay Law—the more capable the model, the simpler the required Harness. Finally, it posits that the new paradigm for engineers is 'Human Steer, Agents Execute,' shifting their core responsibility from writing code to setting direction, building frameworks, and making judgments.

## Reusable Principle

This article details the three evolutions of AI engineering: Prompt, Context, and Harness Engineering. It reveals how OpenAI's team leveraged Harness Engineering to generate nearly a million lines of production code, highlighting the paradigm shift for engineers from coding to designing reliable AI systems. This offers critical insights for adapting to the AI era.
