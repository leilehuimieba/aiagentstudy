## Core Takeaway

Dan Shipper, CEO of Every, argues that AI automation paradoxically increases the demand for expert human work, rather than eliminating it. Drawing on internal experience at Every, he describes two modes of working with agents: agent employees (coworker and embedded) and human-AI collaboration in tools like Codex and Claude Code. He explains that AI commoditizes the residue of past human competence, making it cheap and abundant. This abundance creates sameness (slop), which in turn creates demand for differentiated, high-status work that only humans can provide. He addresses the objection of exponential benchmark progress by arguing that benchmarks measure performance inside a fixed frame, and saturating one frame simply creates new demand for experts who can adapt that competence to novel, live problems. The essay concludes that the future of knowledge work is not replacement, but a continuous cycle of automation creating new, higher-value human roles.

## Reusable Principle

This piece delves into the paradox of AI automation, asserting that making competence cheap paradoxically creates more demand for human experts. It explains how humans continuously shift the “frame” of valuable work, staying ahead of AI's benchmark-driven progress. The author argues that true “agency”—the ability to want for oneself—remains a uniquely human trait, distinguishing us from even the most advanced AI agents.
