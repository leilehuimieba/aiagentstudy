内容简介
Master AI Video Marketing w/ Magnific CEO Joaquín Cuenca Abela
The Shift to AI-Driven Creativity

Jason Calacanis introduces a new installment of the AI Basics series, emphasizing the mantra of building things "better, cheaper, faster." In the creative realm, AI is no longer just about doing the same tasks for less money; it's about exploring the "void"—discovering new creative possibilities that were previously impossible or too expensive to pursue.

Demo: Post-Apocalyptic San Francisco in 24 Hours

Joaquín Cuenca Abela, the CEO of Magnific, demonstrates the power of his platform by showing a cinematic trailer he produced alone in just 24 hours. The video features a dystopian San Francisco with a tattered "This Week in Startups" logo and a character modeled after Jason Calacanis.

Key takeaways from the demo process:

Storyboarding: Using AI to generate and connect multiple images into a cohesive narrative.
Prompting vs. Reference: Using a mix of text prompts and reference images to guide the AI when precision is needed.
Efficiency: Transforming a process that would traditionally cost $100,000 into a project costing roughly $1 per second of finished video.
Impact on Jobs and Hollywood

Contrary to fears of job displacement, Joaquín argues that AI is a tool that raises both the "floor" and the "ceiling" for creators. Major Hollywood studios are already using these tools to greenlight projects that were previously too expensive, thereby creating hundreds of jobs for crews and actors that would not have existed otherwise.

The Future: Hyper-Personalization

As compute power increases, the industry is moving toward a future where advertisements and media can be dynamically generated. This includes:

Localization: Automatically changing backgrounds or languages based on the viewer's region.
Bespoke Content: Creating hundreds of versions of a single ad concept to target specific demographics in real-time.
Conclusion

While AI is rapidly evolving and becoming a standard tool in marketing and filmmaking, Joaquín emphasizes that the "alchemy" of creativity still requires human individuality. The demand for people who can tell meaningful stories and inject their unique experiences into their work is expected to rise as the tools for execution become more accessible.
