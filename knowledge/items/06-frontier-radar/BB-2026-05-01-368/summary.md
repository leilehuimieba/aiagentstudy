## Core Takeaway

Using the contrasting events of Xiaomi's profit plunge and Micron's market cap hitting one trillion on May 26, 2026, as a starting point, this article delves into the intense divergence currently unfolding in the memory chip industry. The core argument is that AI-driven memory demand (HBM, server DDR5) and consumer-grade memory (smartphones, PCs) have formed a three-tiered, diverging market. The article focuses on the new narrative of Long-Term Agreements (LTAs), arguing that 3-5 year fixed-price contracts signed by cloud providers to secure AI memory capacity could fundamentally alter the cyclical nature of the memory industry, shifting memory stocks from cyclical valuations to infrastructure utility valuations. However, the author remains cautious, pointing out that the premise for LTAs to eliminate cyclicality is that AI demand does not collapse. It warns that Micron's current high valuation is based on peak-cycle earnings, posing a risk of a Davis Double Play. The article also highlights the significant market disagreement over a memory super-cycle, using the example of Goldman Sachs missing out on Micron's rally.
