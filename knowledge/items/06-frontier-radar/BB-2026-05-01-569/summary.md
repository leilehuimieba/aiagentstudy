## Core Takeaway

This essay by Audrey Wollen, published in The Yale Review, is a sophisticated critique of the pervasive narrative that the 21st century is a cultural wasteland. Wollen uses W. David Marx's book 'Blank Space: A Cultural History of the Twenty-First Century' as a springboard to challenge the idea that a lack of 'newness' equates to a lack of cultural value. She argues that critics like Marx, Alex Ross, and Mark Fisher are engaging in a form of nostalgia for a semi-mythical past. Wollen contends that the current era is not empty but is instead characterized by a different kind of cultural production: one of repetition, re-performance, and kitsch. She explores concepts like 'retromania' and 'hauntology' to show how artists are using the recycled, the restaged, and the spectral to create meaning. Through a deep analysis of the band Purity's cover of hardcore punk songs, Wollen demonstrates how re-performance can transform kitsch into a new, emotionally resonant form of subculture. She concludes that the 'blank space' is not an absence but a different kind of presence, a marshland teeming with life, where the act of repetition itself becomes a form of critique and a way to navigate a world burdened by history and precarity.
