## Core Takeaway

The author dissects a single footnote from Freud's 'Civilization and Its Discontents' where he speculates that primitive man domesticated fire by renouncing the urge to urinate on it, a sexual act of homosexual rivalry. The critique is devastating: the premise is empirically false (modern humans and primates show no such urge), the logic is flawed (wildfires are not small, isolated flames that a single act of urination could 'spare'), and the methodology is non-scientific. The author contrasts Freud's 'oraculation'—consulting his own genius in isolation from data—with the scientific process of logical assessment, which involves identifying and testing premises and implications. The article concludes that Freud's work, revered by a cult-like following, is a product of self-delusion and a cargo cult mentality, where people mistake the trappings of genius for genuine insight. The piece is a passionate and rigorous defense of scientific rationality against intellectual charlatanism.
