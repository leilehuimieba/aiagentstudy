## Core Takeaway

Using the commercialization paths of OpenAI and Anthropic as comparative examples, the article systematically analyzes the fundamental contradictions AI faces in monetization. It points out that the advertising model is constrained by the low proportion of commercializable queries in AI conversations (only 1/5 to 1/4 of traditional search), the limited number of ads that can be embedded in a single response (only 1/3 to 1/2 of traditional search), and an invisible ceiling of user trust. The subscription model, on the other hand, grapples with a cost structure dilemma: the marginal cost of AI inference is non-zero and non-negligible, leading to structural losses under a 'low-price subscription + heavy user' model in the Agent era. The article argues that the future of AI commercialization is not a binary choice between 'subscription or advertising,' but rather a shift from 'selling access' to 'selling outcomes.' This involves building business models around deep services such as enterprise workflows, platform-level services, and transaction commissions. It concludes that as Agents begin to complete tasks for users, the value of AI will shift from 'what was answered' to 'what was accomplished.'
