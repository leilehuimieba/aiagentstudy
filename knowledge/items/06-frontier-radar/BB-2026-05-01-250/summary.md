## Core Takeaway

In this annotated lightning talk from PyCon US 2026, Simon Willison covers the key LLM developments from November 2025 to May 2026. He identifies November 2025 as a critical inflection point, particularly for coding. The 'best' model title changed hands five times among Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google. The real breakthrough was that coding agents, powered by Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards, crossed a quality barrier from 'often-work' to 'mostly-work,' becoming reliable daily drivers. The talk also covers the rise of 'Claws' (personal AI assistants), exemplified by the OpenClaw project, which started as an obscure repo in November and took the world by storm by February. Other highlights include Google's Gemini 3.1 Pro producing remarkably good SVG illustrations, the release of capable open-weight models like Google's Gemma 4 and China's GLM-5.1 and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, the latter of which ran on a laptop and outperformed a frontier model on a specific creative task. Willison concludes that the two main themes are the maturation of coding agents and the surprising performance of local, laptop-available models.
