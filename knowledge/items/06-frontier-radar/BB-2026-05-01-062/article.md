100 Years of Progress in 100 Days: Sequoia AI Ascent 2026 Keynote
The Shift from Communication to Computation

The 2026 Sequoia AI Ascent keynote marks a fundamental shift in how we perceive technological progress. While previous waves like the internet and mobile were revolutions in communication (how information is distributed), AI represents a revolution in computation (how information is processed). This shift is additive, building upon decades of transistor and network evolution to reach a point where AI is performing tasks indistinguishable from magic.

The $10 Trillion Services Opportunity

For the first time, a technology wave is addressing both software and services. The Total Addressable Market (TAM) for services is estimated at a staggering $10 trillion. This transition means that AI isn't just a tool to make workers more productive; it is becoming the worker.

The MAD Strategy for AI Builders

To navigate this, Sequoia suggests the MAD framework:

Moats: Focus on 'customer-back' integration. Capabilities change daily, but customer relationships are durable.
Affordance: Create 'brain-dead simple' interfaces. High power (like a terminal) lacks affordance for the average user; tools should work like a hammer.
Diffusion: Capitalize on the gap between what foundation models can do and how slowly the Fortune 500 adopts them.
The Era of Functional Agents

In 2022, agents like AutoGPT were 'cute but useless.' By 2026, we have reached a stage where agents are functional, persistent, and autonomous. An agent is defined by three pillars: Reasoning/Planning, Taking Action, and Iteration.

We are moving up a scale of 'agentness'—from tab autocompletion to 'dark factories' where human review is removed entirely. This leads to the era of Services as Software, where tokens replace salaries in fields ranging from legal litigation to genomic medicine.

The Cognitive Industrial Revolution

Constantine draws a parallel between physical work and cognitive work. Just as machines now perform 99.9% of physical labor, AI is on track to perform 99.9% of cognition.

The Aluminum Analogy: Intelligence is becoming a commodity. Just as electrolysis made precious aluminum common, AI makes high-level expertise disposable.
Alien Design: AI-driven optimization will lead to designs that are more efficient but non-intuitive to the human mind.
Emerging Sciences: We are currently in the 'tinkering' phase of AI, but a fundamental science of cognition—comparable to thermodynamics—is expected to emerge.
The Human Measure

Despite the rapid displacement of cognitive labor, the keynote concludes with a reminder that value is not found in a vacuum. As Protagoras said, "Man is the measure of all things." While AI can do the work, only human relationships and the 'measure of the heart' provide the reason to care. In a world where 100 years of progress happens in 100 days, the only constant is human connection.
