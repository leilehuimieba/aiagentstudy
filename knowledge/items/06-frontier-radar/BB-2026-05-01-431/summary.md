## Core Takeaway

Ben Thompson examines SpaceX's IPO filing, which seeks a $2 trillion valuation on $18.67 billion in revenue and $4.9 billion in losses, driven by a claimed $28.5 trillion TAM that includes $26.5 trillion from AI. He draws parallels to Tesla's history of meme-driven valuation, where Musk's ability to sell a dream—Mars, self-driving, unlimited compute—has repeatedly transformed shared delusion into market reality. The core of the article makes a detailed technical case for data centers in space: not as massive terrestrial-like buildings, but as individual satellite racks (like Starlink V2 Minis) interconnected with lasers, powered by solar panels, and cooled by radiators. Thompson argues that the primary use case is agentic inference, where latency is less critical than capacity, making space-based compute viable. He also identifies a 'stick': terrestrial data centers face growing zoning and power constraints, which could make space the only long-term option. While acknowledging the massive risks and assumptions, he concludes the IPO is a return to what an IPO should be—funding ambitious, speculative ventures—and welcomes Musk's alternative path to unlimited compute.
