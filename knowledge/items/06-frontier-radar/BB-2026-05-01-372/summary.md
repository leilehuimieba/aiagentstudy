## Core Takeaway

Tencent Research Institute, in collaboration with the Institute of Population and Labor Economics at the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, published the 'Big Data Research Report on New AI Career Trends.' Based on approximately 100 million public job listings from six major domestic recruitment platforms between Q1 2024 and Q2 2025, the report reveals five core findings. First, the penetration rate of AI-related jobs is about 1.6%-1.92%, on par with developed countries like the US, but the implicit penetration is deeper. The release of models like DeepSeek has acted as a catalyst for pulsed growth in job demand. Second, the growth in AI jobs is shifting from building tools to using them. The proportion of application-oriented roles has surged from less than 20% to 35% within a year. Among non-technical roles, AI penetration is growing fastest in consulting/analyst and design/creative categories. Third, unlike the US market where entry-level positions are shrinking, entry-level roles in China remain resilient due to differences in cost structures and industrial stages, though this may only be a temporary window. Fourth, AI jobs heavily favor highly educated and experienced professionals, with 71% requiring a bachelor's degree or higher and an average experience requirement 1.6-2.2 years above the market average. However, the signaling value of degrees is depreciating, with AI skills becoming an alternative credential. Fifth, the average salary premium for AI jobs remains above 40% and is more resilient, with high-salary ranges declining only half as much as the overall market during downturns. The report concludes that AI's impact on employment is not simply about job creation or destruction, but a rewriting of the logic of occupational stratification, requiring individuals, businesses, and education systems to adapt anew.
