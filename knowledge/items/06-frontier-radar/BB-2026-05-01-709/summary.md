## Core Takeaway

This article is an in-depth investigative report from Sanlian Life Weekly. Through interviews with multiple teenagers who were sent to internet addiction treatment or special training institutions in Henan and other places, their parents, former instructors, psychological counselors, and scholars, it comprehensively reveals the dark side of the 'juvenile behavioral correction' industry with annual revenue exceeding 70 billion yuan. The article starts with specific cases—14-year-old Wu Rong was tricked by her parents into 'Lixuan Education' under the pretext of a police investigation, where she underwent 540 days of military-style management and ruler-based corporal punishment; 13-year-old Liu Jiahao suffered physical abuse at the 'Yashengsi Quality Education Base', including running with a tire around his body. The report traces the evolution of the industry: from Yang Yongxin's electroshock therapy to traditional Chinese studies academies and special training camps, and to the current form of education consulting companies and family education courses that attract customers through short-video platforms. Parents, anxious about their children's school aversion, rebelliousness, and gaming addiction, pay high fees (30,000 to 50,000 yuan per year) for 'quick correction' services, but the institutions often respond with violence, brainwashing, and fabricated videos. Scholars and lawyers point out that these institutions generally lack proper qualifications for education, medical care, and accommodation, and supervision is severely lacking. Many victims say such experiences leave long-term psychological trauma and further deteriorate their relationship with their parents, but parents are often unaware of this.
