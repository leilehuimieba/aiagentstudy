## Core Takeaway

This article from Aleph Neuro describes a milestone in brain imaging: using ultrasound and microbubbles to create the most detailed vascular image of a living human brain through the skull, without surgery. The technique exploits the neurovascular coupling—active neurons trigger blood flow changes—and sends ultrasound waves through the skull to map blood flow from red blood cells or injected microbubbles. By localizing millions of microbubble centroids, super-resolution is achieved, beating the diffraction limit. The resulting 3D image shows vessels as small as arterioles, with resolution 100 times greater volumetrically than comparable CT. The company also open-sources the processing pipeline and dataset, aiming to advance applications in stroke, Alzheimer's, and traumatic brain injury. Future work targets contrast-free imaging using machine learning to recover weak signals from red blood cells alone.
