## Core Takeaway

The article builds a comprehensive case for physical media ownership by dissecting the nature of digital transactions. It explains that most 'purchases' are actually revocable licenses, citing legal cases and consumer lawsuits. It then catalogs numerous instances where digital content was removed from users' libraries due to license expirations, service shutdowns, or platform policy changes—including examples from Disney+, HBO Max, PlayStation Store, Nintendo eShop, and Google Stadia. The piece also compares streaming quality (bitrate, compression artifacts, lossy audio) unfavorably to Blu-ray, highlights subscription price hikes, and documents cases of remote deletion (e.g., Amazon's removal of Orwell's *1984*) and post-release alterations (e.g., Disney+ edits to Star Wars). The conclusion is implicit: physical media gives the buyer actual control, resale value, and offline access that digital licenses cannot match.
