## Core Takeaway

This article is an in-depth interview by Z Potentials with Andrew Dai, former Research Scientist and Director at Google DeepMind. Andrew spent over 14 years at Google, deeply involved in the evolution of large models from PaLM to Gemini, and co-authored foundational papers on language model pre-training, supervised fine-tuning, and MoE architecture. In the interview, he recalls the innovative culture of the early 40-person Google Brain team and the pivotal transition of PaLM 2 from a research project to a deployed product. He shares the logic behind leaving Google to found Elorian AI: open-source models lower the barrier to entry, text capabilities have matured, and visual reasoning represents a vast, under-explored market. Andrew introduces the concept of the 'jagged frontier,' arguing that AI capabilities do not grow linearly; instead, they far surpass humans in some areas while remaining fragile in basic abilities like counting and spatial understanding. He criticizes current large models for essentially reasoning within 'text space' and lacking true 'world understanding,' predicting a future of 'specialized Frontier Models' where visual reasoning and multimodal understanding are core to the next generation of AI.
