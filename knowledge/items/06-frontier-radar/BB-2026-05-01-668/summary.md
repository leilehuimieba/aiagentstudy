## Core Takeaway

Drawing on the two-falls theodicy and the Angelic Fall as a metaphor, the author explores how irreversible choices by sufficiently capable beings can permanently distort the world. The essay then traces the epistemological divergence between Islamic scholasticism (which abandoned natural philosophy after al-Ghazālī's occasionalism) and the Latin West, which preserved the belief that the world is both knowable and good—until the Enlightenment eroded the latter pillar. Without a metaphysical telos, modernity has replaced goodness with efficiency and utility, leading to instrumental rationality that can justify atrocities (Buck v. Bell, the Holocaust) while still enabling material progress. The third section addresses common criticisms of LLMs—stochastic parrots, environmental footprint, intellectual property, and hallucination—arguing they either mischaracterize the technology or distract from the core alignment challenge. The author contends that the real danger is the growing divergence between capability and the good, where small deviations in powerful systems cascade into irreversible harm.
