## Core Takeaway

This New Yorker interview with 2025 Nobel literature laureate László Krasznahorkai, conducted by Merve Emre, delves deep into his creative philosophy. Krasznahorkai explains how his notoriously long, spiraling sentences emerged from a desire to mimic „explosive confession“ in spoken language, an approach he developed after years of dissatisfaction with his own work. He reveals that he considers nearly all his books failures, a perfectionism that drives him to write. The conversation explores his central theme: the loss of a transcendent, sacred relationship to beauty in a disenchanted, pleasure-driven technical age. Krasznahorkai laments that while beauty endures (Michelangelo, the Acropolis), fewer people can truly experience it. He critiques the commodification of culture and the rise of traditionalist populism. The interview offers a rare, intimate portrait of a major literary figure's mind — his influences (Dostoevsky, Bach, Japanese art), his creative process (pacing rooms, composing sentences internally), and his melancholic yet sharp diagnosis of modern cultural crisis.
