## Core Takeaway

Using PingCAP CTO Huang Dongxu's db9 project as a starting point, this article reveals the astonishing capabilities of AI Agents in software development: completing a database project with 1 million lines of Rust code in three months with zero human coding. Centered around the premise that "AI can write anything," the article discusses the phenomenon of tech stack lock-ins (language, framework, database) being broken—Bun migrated from Zig to Rust in just 6 days, and enterprises can use Agents to rewrite applications at any time. It then explores the trend of "rewriting," including the restoration of sourceless systems (the Thoughtworks case, the rebuilding of a 30-year-old MUD game), and points out that what truly matters is the knowledge within the software rather than the code itself. Xu Hao emphasizes knowledge engineering, arguing that the essence of development is specifying specifications and acceptance criteria, with AI acting merely as a "compiler" to transform the carrier. The article finally focuses on human value: the steering and judgment of senior engineers ("veterans") are irreplaceable, while enterprise software maintenance remains a pain point, as AI-generated code might introduce more bugs after deployment. The entire article features exclusive interviews with multiple experts, offering substantial information and sharp insights.
