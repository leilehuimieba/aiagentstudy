## Core Takeaway

The article analyzes the competitive balance of the 2026 World Cup after its expansion to 48 teams. It presents data showing the tournament has the lowest average FIFA ranking (32.4) on record and a higher average winning margin (2.3 goals) than previous group stages. However, the author argues that these surface-level statistics mask compelling underdog stories, such as Cape Verde holding Spain and Uruguay to draws, and Curacao drawing with Ecuador after a heavy loss. The analysis uses historical comparisons to show that underdogs have always been competitive in World Cups, avoiding defeat roughly once every three encounters. While the expanded format creates more ranking mismatches, the rate of upset wins has not dramatically increased; most surprises are draws. The article concludes that fears of minnows being overwhelmed have proved unfounded, and the class of 2026 continues a tradition of spirited resistance.
