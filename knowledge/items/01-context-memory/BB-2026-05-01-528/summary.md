## Core Takeaway

This article is a transcript of a dialogue from the 2026 Utopia Reader's Day event. Chen Jiaying, from a philosophical perspective, explores the relationship between cognition and being-in-the-world, arguing that cognition stems from desire and can only be formed within specific contexts, with objectivity being 'a series of increasingly pure efforts.' Luo Xin responds from a historical viewpoint, emphasizing that historical research pursues truth while acknowledging its own limitations, and that the greatest value of the humanities lies in the process—cultivating judgment and the ability to ask questions. Zhou Sicheng raises the sharp question of whether AI can achieve 'the most objective cognition without being-in-the-world.' The three scholars agree that AI can provide brilliant answers and synthesize existing knowledge, but cannot truly pose new questions. In an era of AI as a 'deus ex machina,' the core value of philosophy and history lies in cultivating the human ability to ask questions, maintain judgment, and build one's own narrative from fragments. The dialogue also touches on the plurality of historical interpretation, the differences in objectivity between the social and natural sciences, and the shortcomings of Chinese education in terms of intellectual depth.
