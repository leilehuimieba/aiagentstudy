## Core Takeaway

The article focuses on MiniMax's latest open-source flagship model, M3, evaluating its three core capabilities: long context, multimodality, and coding. The author designed four hands-on tasks: independently reproducing an ICLR 2025 award-winning paper (12 hours without human intervention), identifying the 74 DSX ecosystem enterprise logos from Jensen Huang's Computex keynote PPT and creating an interactive webpage, making a food check-in map based on Jensen Huang's Beijing itinerary, and understanding a linguistics olympiad explanation video to reproduce a problem-solving webpage. All tasks were completed successfully, demonstrating M3's comprehensive abilities in long-form autonomous programming, multimodal understanding, information retrieval, and webpage generation. The article also introduces key technologies behind M3: the MSA sparse attention mechanism (reducing per-token computation to 1/20th of the previous generation at 1M context), an interactive user simulator framework (used for training coding and agent capabilities), and a native multimodal approach that mixes text and images from the first step of pre-training. Finally, the article points out that M3 is the first open-source model to simultaneously possess these three capabilities, with comprehensive performance approaching top-tier closed-source models like Claude Opus and GPT-5.5, offering outstanding cost-effectiveness.
