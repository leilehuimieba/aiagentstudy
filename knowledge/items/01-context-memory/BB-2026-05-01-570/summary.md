## Core Takeaway

The author, a veteran software engineer, draws a parallel between the old, discredited practice of measuring developer productivity by lines of code and the current trend of AI vendors touting volume-based metrics like '75% of new code is AI-generated.' He contrasts these with earlier, more meaningful outcome claims like GitHub's '55% faster task completion.' The article reviews the complex and often contradictory research on AI's productivity impact, including studies showing gains for juniors, increased code churn, and even slower performance for experienced devs in their own codebases. It critiques the rise of 'AI Adoption Maturity Models' as another form of measuring adoption intensity rather than actual value. The author's core concern is that these vanity metrics are being used to justify major business decisions, such as the large-scale layoffs at Block and Atlassian, where AI is cited as the reason for needing fewer people. He argues that if AI truly made everyone more productive, companies would use that capacity to deliver more value, not cut headcount. The article concludes by advocating for an 'AI-first' approach to work but a 'battle-tested' approach to measurement, urging readers to always ask: 'Is that an outcome, or a volume?'
