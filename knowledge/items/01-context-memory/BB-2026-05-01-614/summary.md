## Core Takeaway

This article is a compilation of an in-depth interview from 'Crossroads' with Boris Cherny, head of Claude Code, and Cat Wu, product lead. The article unfolds in a conversational format, reviewing the fundamental changes in the Anthropic team's internal working methods over the past year since Claude Code's launch. The core narrative is that AI coding has undergone a shift from 'prompt engineering' to 'context engineering' to 'loop engineering.' Boris shares his new methodology: instead of prompting the agent one by one, he 'writes a loop and lets it prompt Claude for me,' revealing that roughly half of his engineering work is now done remotely via a phone. Cat shares her practice of 'writing mistakes into CLAUDE.md and letting it verify by running itself,' and marvels at the experience where 'another person's Claude' automatically fixed her bug. The article also discusses trends like 'everyone writes code' (designers, finance using Claude Code), 'auto mode is safer than step-by-step confirmation,' and 'role fusion.' Towards the end, the author organizes Addy Osmani's framework for Loop Engineering, discussing five components (Automations, Worktrees, Skills, Plugins, Sub-agents) and the memory layer (State), and reminds engineers to stay clear-headed with the concepts of 'understanding debt' and 'cognitive surrender.'
