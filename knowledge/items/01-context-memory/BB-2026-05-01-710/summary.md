## Core Takeaway

The article is a compilation by CSDN of a podcast interview with Vlad Feinberg, head of pre-training at Google DeepMind Gemini. Vlad shares how he received an on-the-spot bonus from Jeff Dean for solving Bard's compiler and hyperparameter issues, emphasizing the core value of 'doing the dirty work' engineering capabilities in AI competition. He reveals that Gemini 2.0 Flash was trained by just a 5-person team working 24-hour shifts between Silicon Valley and Paris for 40 consecutive days without sleep. He also exposes that the Wall Street Journal deliberately omitted Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking, which topped the LMSYS leaderboard, when comparing with DeepSeek to create an 'open-source surpassing' narrative. The article delves into the essence of pre-training research—designing a 'recipe' for predictable scaling laws—and underlying technical principles such as quantization, distillation, and pipeline prefill. Vlad uses the example that 'AI cannot have its lawyer license revoked' to illustrate human irreplaceability, and points out that ordinary engineers can transition to cutting-edge teams by helping LLMs land in practice and solving inference overhead through real-world accumulation. He also gives specific job-seeking advice: contribute to open-source projects like vLLM or TensorRT, implement a Transformer from scratch, and record an explanatory video. The full text is dense with information, covering both technical inside stories and career guidance.
