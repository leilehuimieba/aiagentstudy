## Core Takeaway

The article offers a deep commentary on the incident where Anthropic's Fable 5 / Mythos 5 was restricted by the US government on national security grounds. The author first clarifies the nature of the event: it was not a routine API adjustment but an export control directive issued by the government based on national security authority, involving Amazon's multiple roles as an investor, cloud platform provider, and security auditor. The article criticizes the open-source camp for seizing the opportunity to promote a simplified narrative of 'closed-source is unreliable, open-source is nobler,' pointing out its logical fallacy of equating 'accessibility' with 'substitutability.' The author emphasizes that for models like Fable 5, which touch the boundaries of high-risk capabilities such as cybersecurity and biological research, the value of open source cannot substitute for equivalent capability and safety governance. The article further analyzes the nuanced role of Amazon: as Anthropic's largest investor and cloud platform partner, yet also a key node that triggered the government's risk assessment, it reveals that the authority to release frontier AI models is shifting from unilateral company decisions to multi-stakeholder governance. Finally, the author proposes a future landscape of tiered model architectures and points out that open source is a foundational asset, not a fig leaf; the true replacement for Fable can only be another model that is equally powerful and equally serious about safety governance.
