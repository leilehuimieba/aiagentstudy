## Core Takeaway

This is the third article in the "AI Faith and Bubble" series, focusing on the systemic impact of the Silicon Valley Seven's projected $700 billion AI capital expenditure in 2026. The article unfolds on three levels: First, the supply chain level, where consumer electronics giants like Apple, Xiaomi, and Huawei are under pressure as TSMC's capacity is squeezed by AI computing power vendors like NVIDIA and storage prices surge, forcing them to raise prices or cut product lines, with profit distribution heavily skewed. Second, the energy level, where the surging electricity demand from AI data centers conflicts with aging grid infrastructure. Tech giants are building their own power plants and competing for electricity, leading to higher residential electricity bills and triggering environmental and community issues. Third, the financial level, where tech giants use off-balance-sheet financing tools like SPVs and REITs to transfer debt risks to external capital such as pension funds and insurance companies, creating an artificial financial asset pipeline with risks layered and hidden. The article cites warnings from industry insiders, research institutions, and Michael Burry, the real-life inspiration for "The Big Short," revealing the structural risks and cost socialization behind the AI boom.
