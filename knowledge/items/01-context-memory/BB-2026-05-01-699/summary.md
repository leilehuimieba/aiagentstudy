## Core Takeaway

ProPublica and Oregon Public Broadcasting analyze satellite data and state records to expose the inequities of Oregon's water allocation system. The Deschutes River is heavily diverted by the Central Oregon Irrigation District (COID), which holds senior water rights from the early 1900s. During a historic drought, only 26% of the diverted water was consumed by crops—mostly grass and pasture—while the rest leaked, evaporated, or ran off. Meanwhile, downstream farmers like Chris Casad in Jefferson County were forced to fallow fields and face financial ruin. The article traces the legal doctrine of 'beneficial use' and 'waste,' showing how loosely defined terms allow wealthy landowners—including Phil Knight's horse ranch and a luxury estate sold for $4.8 million—to maintain green lawns and low-value crops. The state has rarely canceled water rights, and COID actively monitors to ensure landowners use water at least once every five years to avoid losing rights. The report combines data visualizations, historical context, personal stories, and expert interviews to argue that the system prioritizes property rights over ecological health and economic fairness.
