## Core Takeaway

This paper presents the largest empirical study of algorithmic hiring to date, analyzing data from 3.4 million real applicants submitting 4 million applications to 156 employers across 11 sectors, all assessed by algorithms from a single vendor. The study introduces and validates the concept of 'algorithmic monoculture' in hiring, demonstrating two key findings. First, it reveals large-scale adverse impact against Asian and Black applicants when analyzed at the individual position level, as required by U.S. employment law (Title VII), which is masked in aggregate analyses. Second, it provides the first empirical evidence of systemic rejections in deployed hiring algorithms, where applicants are rejected across all positions at a rate significantly exceeding what would be expected from statistically independent decisions. The authors contextualize their findings by showing that the baseline of independent decisions accurately predicts rejection rates in other hiring data without centralized algorithmic assessment. Policy recommendations include measuring adverse impact per position, strengthening market surveillance, monitoring algorithmic monoculture, and expanding researcher access to hiring platform data.
