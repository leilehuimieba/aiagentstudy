## Core Takeaway

Using the release of the OpenAI Codex usage report as a starting point, and set against the backdrop of the U.S. government requesting restrictions on the release of GPT-5.6, this article provides an in-depth analysis of how the AI agent product Codex is transforming the way we work. Report data shows: 97.9% of active OpenAI employees use Codex, with over 90% of their workload completed by Codex on average; among external organizations, the usage by non-developers has grown by over a hundredfold, expanding Codex from a developer tool to roles in legal, recruiting, and operations; users are submitting increasingly heavy tasks (the proportion of tasks that would take a skilled human over 1 hour rose from 35% to 70%), and running multiple agents concurrently has become the norm (peaking at 71 hours of daily runtime); users are beginning to accumulate reusable workflows (skill usage rate climbed to 26.6%). The article points out that as AI shifts from conversation to execution, risks enter real workflows and regulatory sensitivity rises accordingly—the phased release of GPT-5.6 is a direct reflection of this logic.
