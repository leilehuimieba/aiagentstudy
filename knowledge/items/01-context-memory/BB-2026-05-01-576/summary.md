## Core Takeaway

Using the explosive growth of Hong Kong's insurance market in 2025 as an entry point, the article notes that behind the 50.6% year-on-year premium growth, cross-border sales and 'shadow referrers' have become increasingly rampant. The author details the mechanics of 'order-poaching': licensed agents privately refer policies to brokerage firms to obtain 'referral fees' exceeding standard commissions, thereby bypassing their insurance companies' oversight. Whistleblower materials obtained by Caixin involving over 1,300 policies show that the agents involved came from leading insurers including AIA, Prudential, and China Life, with estimated policy amounts reaching HK$200 million. The article also clarifies the role distinctions among agents, brokers, and referrers, and cites industry perspectives calling for regulatory intervention to curb ecosystem deterioration.
