## Core Takeaway

This article argues that the long-standing T-shaped model for UX professionals—deep expertise in one area with enough breadth to collaborate—is being dismantled by AI. The author contends that AI has made the cost of acquiring breadth near zero, collapsing the relay race of specialists (researcher, designer, engineer) into a single, end-to-end workflow. Drawing on survey data (91% of designers use AI weekly, 84% of developers use AI tools) and historical parallels (desktop publishing), the piece makes the case for the 'polymath architect': a professional who retains deep vertical skill (judgment, quality bar) but expands their horizontal range to direct AI across the entire product stack. The article provides actionable advice for specialists, including mapping handoffs, shipping outside one's lane, encoding judgment into tools, and guarding against skill atrophy. It concludes that depth still matters critically for quality control, but it must be stacked on top of a much wider breadth of capability.
