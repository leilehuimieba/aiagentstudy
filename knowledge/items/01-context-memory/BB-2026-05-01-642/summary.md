## Core Takeaway

The article extends Team Topologies (Skelton & Pais) to the context of AI agents producing applications. It argues that cognitive load in agentic production does not disappear but compresses onto a single person, becoming an "anticipation burden" and "cognitive throughput" problem. The agentic platform absorbs part of this burden by making itself queryable by agents. The author adapts the four team types: stream-aligned teams become non-technical business teams; the platform team provides systemic context, guardrails, and tooling as self-service; enabling teams temporarily bridge gaps; complicated subsystem teams handle deep AI infrastructure. Three interaction modes—facilitating, X-as-a-Service, collaboration—evolve as teams and platform mature. Governance (shadow IT prevention) and a graduation path (rule of three for guardrail systemization) are proposed to make the model sustainable. The article concludes that business teams can become stream-aligned without coding, as the platform absorbs technical complexity, while developers shift to building the platform.
