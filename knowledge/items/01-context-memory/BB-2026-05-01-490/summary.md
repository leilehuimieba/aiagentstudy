## Core Takeaway

The article uses the 1994 World Cup in the United States as a historical anchor to compare and forecast the 2026 World Cup. The author points out that the 2026 World Cup will be highly industrialized and commercialized: the expansion to 48 teams and 104 matches aims to reach more people and monetize traffic; the convergence of global youth training leads to tactical modularity, with high pressing and space utilization becoming a universal language; short videos and streaming services break the game into fragmented content, replacing traditional complete narratives. At the same time, the article also focuses on the final World Cup for veterans like Messi and Ronaldo, as well as the impact of new generations like Mbappé and Kane. The author argues that despite the increasing precision of the football industry, the World Cup's unpredictability and myth-making ability—those unexpected upsets and legends—remain its most captivating core. The article concludes by recalling the viewing experience of each World Cup from a fan's perspective, emphasizing that the charm of the World Cup lies in the fact that it never follows a preset script.
