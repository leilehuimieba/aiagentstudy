## Core Takeaway

This article is an in-depth piece published by the Anthropic Institute, originally titled "When AI builds itself." It systematically argues that AI systems are accelerating their involvement in the AI R&D pipeline, from writing code and running experiments to conducting evaluations, pointing towards the potential for recursive self-improvement in the future. The article is structured into several parts: first, it demonstrates the rapid capability improvement of models on external benchmarks (SWE-bench, CORE-Bench, METR long-horizon tasks); then, it reveals internal Anthropic data, including Claude writing over 80% of production code, an 8x increase in engineer per-capita output, and Claude's success rate on open-ended research tasks rising from 26% to 76%; it then discusses the shift in the human role from executor to direction-setter; finally, it proposes three future scenarios: trend stagnation, compound efficiency gains, and full recursive self-improvement, and explores the possibility of coordinated slowdown. The article is rich in data, rigorous in argumentation, and its analysis of future impacts is measured and boundary-aware.
