## Core Takeaway

The article centers on the core question of 'what can offset the impact of global aging,' examining four mainstream viewpoints one by one: automation and AI, increasing labor force participation among the elderly, international immigration, and the demographic dividends of India and Africa. The author argues that automation has limited effectiveness in labor-intensive fields like elderly care, and the regions most in need of automation are ironically emerging economies with less severe demographic pressures. While elderly labor force participation is rising, its future growth is constrained by factors such as pension systems and the increasing proportion of the very old. The scale of immigration is far too small to fill the labor gaps in aging economies and faces political resistance. Regarding India and Africa, the author acknowledges their large labor forces but points out that India is hampered by weak administrative capital, democratic checks and balances, and internal friction, making it difficult to replicate China's mobilization-driven growth. Africa, due to geographic fragmentation, declining governance scores, and insufficient human capital, also cannot serve as an effective substitute for the global workforce. The article concludes that none of these pathways can reverse the economic impact of demographic shifts, which will lead to higher inflation and slower growth.
