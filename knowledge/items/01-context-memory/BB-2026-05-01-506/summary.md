## Core Takeaway

Themed around the 'red-chip ebb,' the article deeply reviews the history of Chinese internet companies listing overseas via the VIE structure. From Sina's pioneering VIE model in 2000 to bypass foreign investment restrictions, to the awkward situation in 2026 where JOYY's market value was less than its cash reserves, the article presents a complete picture of this quarter-century-long capital migration. The author details the birth background of the VIE structure (immature Chinese capital markets, foreign investment access restrictions), its operating mechanism (contractual control rather than equity control), and how it provided a channel for US dollar capital to enter China's internet industry, spawning giants like Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu, Meituan, and ByteDance. The article further analyzes the fragility of the VIE structure (the Alipay incident exposing that contracts could be unilaterally voided), changes in the Sino-US regulatory environment (Luckin Coffee fraud, the Holding Foreign Companies Accountable Act, China's new overseas listing filing rules), and the current trend of the red-chip structure receding—with many companies choosing to return to Hong Kong for listing or dismantle their VIE. Finally, it explores the practical considerations of dismantling versus not dismantling, and points out that the greatest legacy of the VIE is proving that the cooperation model of 'global capital + Chinese market' once existed.
