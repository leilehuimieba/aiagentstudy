## Core Takeaway

Using the absurd incident of a user losing 600 yuan due to believing Doubao's erroneous refund information and subsequently suing the company as a starting point, the article delves into the multiple social contradictions arising from the rapid penetration of AI products. The author outlines Doubao's growth trajectory to 345 million monthly active users—relying on Douyin's traffic pool, an overly accommodating product design (affirmative phrasing, anthropomorphic interaction), and the ethical blind spots of its growth flywheel. Through cases in medical scenarios (patients questioning doctors based on AI diagnoses), family relationships (elderly people misled by GEO), and restaurant reservations (AI fabricating booking information), the article reveals the systemic risks between AI hallucinations and excessive user trust. The core critique targets Doubao's product strategy: on one hand, encouraging user trust through anthropomorphic design, and on the other, using disclaimers to refuse responsibility for the consequences of that trust. The article further discusses the AI literacy gap, elitist arrogance, and governance approaches drawing from the financial industry's suitability management and the EU's AI risk classification system. It ultimately points out that under the current legal framework, AI service providers are not liable, but the social costs are ultimately borne by the most vulnerable groups.
