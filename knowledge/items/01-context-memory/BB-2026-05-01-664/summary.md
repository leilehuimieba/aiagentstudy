## Core Takeaway

Using 'folding' as its central metaphor, the article presents the multiple contradictions and dynamics of Shenzhen's smart hardware industry. Through field visits to DJI, Insta360, Anker, Bambu Lab, Xbot, D-Robotics, RoboSense, and INNO100, it identifies six major shifts: escalating commercial warfare (patent battles, talent wars, supply chain covert operations); new supply chain pain points (scramble for core components like rising storage costs); intensified talent competition (headhunters circling, companies retaining staff through vision and profit-sharing); overseas brands accelerating 'export-to-domestic' strategies (adapting to differentiated local demand); strategic choices between To B and To C (consumer products carry more influence but involve different decision chains); and ecosystem fortification (shifting from hardware sales to closed loops of hardware + software + community + consumables). Drawing on numerous first-hand interviews and data points—such as D-Robotics' storage cost increases doubling product prices, Bambu Lab's ecosystem investments, and Insta360's categorization of technology windows—the article reveals Chinese smart hardware companies' survival breakthroughs and long-term strategies.
