## Core Takeaway

Justin Chang's review of two new films examines how 'Romería' and 'Rose of Nevada' use coastal landscapes—Spain's northwest shore and a Cornish village—to frame stories of hidden identities, past tragedies, and time travel. Chang highlights Simón's semi-autobiographical tale of an orphaned teen seeking her lineage, filmed with restrained realism that erupts into a poignant flashback sequence, and Jenkin's ghost-ship fable of two men transported to 1993, shot with a spring-wound Bolex camera that evokes cinematic time travel. The review delves into each film's themes of memory, loss, and the possibility of starting over, praising their emotional depth and technical craft while positioning them as a meaningful double bill. Chang's analysis underscores how both directors manipulate time and place to achieve resonant, melancholy mysteries.
