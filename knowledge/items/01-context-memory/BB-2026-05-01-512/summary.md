## Core Takeaway

This article is an in-depth hands-on review of the MiniMax M3 model by AI product domain KOL "AI Product Huang Shu." Instead of focusing on single-point demos, the author designed three realistic long-horizon tasks: 1) Revamping an old AI programming course website into an Agent-Native website, involving multimodal understanding of the old page, restructuring the information architecture, and modifying 2000 lines of code; 2) Building a "Writing Expert Group" using personal knowledge bases, testing style consistency and role boundary stability under multi-role, multi-round feedback; 3) Transforming a Feishu knowledge base into an interactive web-based detective room, testing the model's ability to extract structure from long documents, design levels, and deliver a front-end product. The core conclusion is that M3 truly differentiates itself through stability in the second half of long tasks—it maintains constraints, role boundaries, and task direction as context lengthens and tasks become more complex, unlike weaker models that "start strong like a human but end up like a temp worker taking over." The article also candidly discusses three current boundaries of Agent workflows: long-term stability does not equal full autonomy, trustworthy output is not inherently trustworthy, and building a product does not equal fully understanding the business.
