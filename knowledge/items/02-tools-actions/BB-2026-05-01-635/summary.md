## Core Takeaway

Based on large-scale data from over 70 million job transitions across more than 1,000 occupations in the United States between 2005 and 2019, the article categorizes skills into foundational skills (reading comprehension, basic math, teamwork) and specialized advanced skills (such as blockchain). The study finds that individuals with higher foundational skill scores earn higher wages throughout their careers, get promoted faster, learn new skills more quickly, and adapt better to industry changes. As the half-life of technical skills shrinks from 10 years to 4 years and may drop below 2 years, the importance of foundational skills becomes increasingly prominent. Social skills (communication, empathy, conflict resolution) in particular serve as the glue for efficient team collaboration, and the proportion of management job postings emphasizing collaboration, coaching, and influence has tripled since 2007. The article provides three specific recommendations for leaders: prioritize screening candidates with strong foundational skills during hiring; invest in early-career development to strengthen soft skills; and have managers lead by example to model and reward foundational skill behaviors.
