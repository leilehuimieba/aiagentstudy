## Core Takeaway

The article first points out that AI tools (Vibe Coding) have significantly lowered the barrier to writing code, but what a one-person business truly needs is to 'run a profitable business,' not to 'own an app.' The author uses their own experience (Little Cat Flashlight) to illustrate that writing an app accounts for only 5% of the workload in a product's lifecycle, with the remaining 95% being business aspects like promotion, customer service, and operations. Subsequently, the article provides a detailed demonstration of using the 'CodeFly' product, starting from the prompt 'Create a social app for extroverted cats,' and sequentially completing the entire process: app generation (with built-in products, orders, users, and a data backend), automatic generation of promotional posters and copy by the business assistant, and deployment of an AI customer service agent (supporting WeChat integration). It also verifies the product's applicability in e-commerce scenarios (one-click publishing as a WeChat Mini Program). Finally, the author introduces the concept of 'Vibe Business,' considering CodeFly as the first concrete product in this track, but emphasizes that the three key questions—'what business to do,' 'who to sell to,' and 'how to find those people'—cannot be solved by tools and still require personal judgment and insight.
