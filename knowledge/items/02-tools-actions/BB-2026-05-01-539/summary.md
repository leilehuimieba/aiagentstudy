## Core Takeaway

This article is a critical, first-hand analysis of Google's launch of Antigravity 2.0, a major redesign of its AI IDE. The author, Gergely Orosz, argues the launch is a mess. Key criticisms include: the confusing split into two applications (Antigravity IDE and Antigravity 2.0), the removal of the traditional IDE interface in favor of a conversational agent manager, a buggy and rushed upgrade process that broke the author's setup, poor model support (no Opus 4.7 or GPT 5.5), rapid consumption of Gemini token quotas, and the forced deprecation of the open-source Gemini CLI for a closed-source, incompatible Antigravity CLI. The article highlights a glaring 'Codex' folder in the official launch video, suggesting even Google's own developers prefer a competitor's product. The author concludes that this launch is another example of Google's pattern of breaking developer workflows and failing to become a serious player in the dev tools space.
