## Core Takeaway

This article provides a detailed report on the explosion of Blue Origin's New Glenn rocket during a static fire test on May 28, 2026, and analyzes its cascading impact on Blue Origin, NASA, Amazon, and the entire commercial space industry. The accident destroyed Blue Origin's only launch pad, which is not expected to be operational again until 2028, grounding its heavy-lift rocket for an extended period. This directly impacts NASA's backup plan for the Artemis return-to-the-moon program, forces delays for approximately 30% of Amazon's satellite deployment, and caused a sharp drop in the stock price of AST SpaceMobile, which relies on Blue Origin for launches. The article contrasts this with SpaceX's "unexpected win," noting its upcoming IPO at a $1.75 trillion valuation and its diversified revenue streams from Starlink, defense contracts, and AI data center leases, which create a formidable moat. Finally, it re-evaluates the valuation logic for commercial space companies from a capital market perspective, emphasizing the importance of launch reliability and single-point-of-failure risk.
