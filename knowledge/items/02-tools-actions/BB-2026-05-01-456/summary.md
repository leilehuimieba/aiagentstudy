## Core Takeaway

This article is the 11th installment of the "Token Economics" series, focusing on a key shift in AI software business models: from selling accounts and API calls to selling "completed tasks." Using Intercom's AI customer service bot Fin, which charges $0.99 per resolved customer issue, as a starting point, the article systematically deconstructs four core issues behind the "pay-per-outcome" model: how to define "completion," how to prove contribution, how to calculate costs, and how to allocate responsibility. The author points out that outcome-based AI will make software "heavier" because it requires integrating processes, tools, rules, and verification mechanisms, evolving from a toolbox into an execution system. The article also analyzes the applicable boundaries of this model (high-frequency, clearly defined boundaries, verifiable outcomes) and its unique path in the Chinese market, which may initially appear in non-standardized forms like project-based contracts or performance-based payments. Finally, the article connects the three pricing layers of Token, Action, and Outcome, noting that the price tag is shifting from "paying for capability" to a "list of responsibilities."
