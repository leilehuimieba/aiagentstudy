## Core Takeaway

This article is compiled from an in-depth interview with Dax Raad, co-founder of OpenCode, on The Pragmatic Engineer Podcast. With a candid attitude, Dax systematically reflects on three major problems AI coding tools cause in engineering teams: First, a surge in code output does not mean the product is getting better; feature bloat instead leads to system chaos. Second, AI quietly silences engineers' "guilt," making it too easy to write hacky code and thereby stealing their judgment. Third, most engineers are not using AI to increase output, but to go home earlier with the time saved, which contradicts the "productivity multiplier" narrative vendors sell to CFOs. The article also delves into OpenCode's entrepreneurial journey, product strategy (prioritizing experience and adoption first, then optimizing the underlying layer), business model (inference services and console), and Dax's unique insights on engineering culture, product taste, technical debt cleanup, and career development for engineers. He emphasizes that as AI makes code generation increasingly cheap, what is truly scarce is judgment, restraint, and sensitivity to systemic consequences.
