## Core Takeaway

The author conducted an immediate hands-on and in-depth experience with Claude Opus 4.8 upon its release. The article first introduces the context of the model's launch, viewing it as a rapid firefighting release by Anthropic under pressure from the poor reception of GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.7. The core experience is broken down into several aspects: first, thinking intensity controls are now available to all users, improving controllability; second, the model has become more precise and better at following instructions, but less proactive, no longer guessing users' unstated needs—a boon for professional developers but potentially frustrating for the Vibe Coding community that relies on AI proactiveness; third, the model is more honest, no longer blindly confident in its assertions, with code flaws being four times less likely to slip through compared to the previous generation, and the system card showing a 0% laziness defect rate; fourth, while creative abilities have improved, they still fall short of Opus 4.6, with issues like heavy AI flavor, overuse of metaphors, and parallelism. Additionally, the article mentions the significant price drop for Fast Mode and the new dynamic workflow feature in Claude Code. Finally, the author expresses disappointment over the removal of Opus 4.6 and anticipation for the upcoming, more advanced model, Mythos.
