## Core Takeaway

Using Tesla's cancellation of the Model 2 as a starting point, the article systematically examines the predicament of China's NEV industry from 2024 to the present. Intelligent driving was once the core battleground for differentiation, but after massive R&D investments, automakers found consumers unwilling to pay for it—autonomy became standard equipment without premium pricing. The price war has persisted for three years, spreading from complete vehicles to sales and the supply chain, with stringent sales targets, dealer losses, and suppliers forced below cost. Industry subsidies have exceeded 1.5 trillion yuan, incubating a complete supply chain but failing to prevent overcapacity. Once technology became 'good enough,' NEVs transformed from a world-changing dream into a thin-margin grind. Drawing on extensive practitioner interviews and industry data (BYD's profit decline, Zhongsheng's losses, autonomous driving engineers changing careers), the article captures the industry's shift from entrepreneurial passion to weary resignation, maintaining a cautious outlook on future consolidation.
