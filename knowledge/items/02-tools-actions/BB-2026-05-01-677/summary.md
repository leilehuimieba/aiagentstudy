## Core Takeaway

Taking the game equipment color system as a starting point, the article systematically traces the formation of the color grammar from Diablo to World of Warcraft, pointing out that the hierarchy of gray, white, green, blue, purple, and orange has become a cross-game cognitive model. It then introduces Claude Romano's phenomenology to argue that color in the game world is not a subjective decoration, but a stable way for value to manifest, further proposing the concept of the "game life-world." The third part analyzes how the perceptual differences and cultural associations between colors are utilized to construct a natural value order; the fourth part draws on the desire theory of Deleuze and Guattari to explain how color triggers action chains and group communication, becoming the triggering device of a "desire-assemblage." The conclusion extends the game color system to broader digital interfaces, exploring how it trains players to develop an efficiency-driven, reward-oriented way of seeing, and warning that such rapid judgment reduces the capacity for complex feelings about things. The article closely integrates game design, philosophical theory, and cultural critique, demonstrating solid argumentation and a broad perspective.
