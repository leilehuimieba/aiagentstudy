## Core Takeaway

This article is an in-depth interview by Founder Park with Li Huanzhi, founder of GlobalGPT. Li candidly reviews his transition from a lawyer to an entrepreneur: after the failures of LegalDAO and LegalNow, with only one month of cash runway left, the team created the AI wrapper product GlobalGPT. By aggregating mainstream AI models and offering users access to multiple models with a single subscription, the product capitalized on the market boom of diverse models. Through KOL marketing, SEO, and paid advertising, it achieved over 3 million global users and over $10 million in ARR without any external funding. The article details its growth strategy, user profile (non-tech senior white-collar workers), and the entrepreneurial philosophy that 'being the 1,000th in a red ocean is bigger than being in the top 10 of a small market.' After achieving commercial success, Li was not satisfied with the linear growth of a wrapper business. Driven by concerns that 'AI is widening the gap between elites and ordinary people,' he returned to his original mission and launched Yukie, a service-oriented AI product. Yukie's core concept is that AI should not wait for user commands but should proactively offer services like a personal trainer, helping ordinary people who 'don't know what they don't know' bridge the cognitive gap. The article also explores Yukie's product form, business model, team organization (an AI-native R&D pipeline), and the founder's reflections on balancing mission and business.
