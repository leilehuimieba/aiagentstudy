## Core Takeaway

Kicked off by OpenAI joining forces with 19 PE firms to invest over $4 billion in a deployment company, the article interviews Jove, head of the FDE team at Cresta, and Oliver, VP of Enterprise Business at Invisible Technologies, to provide a comprehensive analysis of the emerging FDE role. Starting from the day-to-day work of an FDE, Jove explains how FDEs turn AI from a demo into enterprise-grade workflows, draws parallels with the Palantir model, and discusses the division of labor between FDEs and FDPMs (Frontline Deployment Product Managers). He emphasizes that FDEs need solid engineering skills, client communication experience, and resilience under pressure, arguing that in the short term, FDEs will not be replaced by AI; instead, they will become more efficient as tools evolve, but the complexity gap will still require human intervention. Oliver, from the perspective of PE and consulting, analyzes the deep-rooted reasons why large model companies are proactively engaging in deployment — to address the pain points of low enterprise AI adoption rates and unfulfilled ROI. He highlights three motivations for PE firms collaborating with model companies: signal value (demonstrating AI leadership to LPs), portfolio value creation, and exposure to high-return assets. He also criticizes the misconception of viewing AI solely as a cost-cutting tool, emphasizing that the true value lies in revenue generation, and provides concrete examples such as an AI sales assistant for an asset management firm and a dairy health report for a dairy company. The article concludes by predicting that the consulting industry will see short-term growth due to AI transformation demand, but long-term value belongs to those companies that can leave behind a transformed business.
