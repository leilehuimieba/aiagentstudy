## Core Takeaway

The essay analyzes SpaceX's $1.77 trillion IPO valuation, the largest in history. It examines the implied growth trajectory: 41.5% annual revenue growth for 15 years to reach $3.4 trillion by 2040. The author reframes this by comparing it to historical growth rates of other giant companies (Tesla, Amazon, Cisco), introducing the concept of a 'growth frontier' — a statistical relationship showing that growth rate declines as starting size increases. SpaceX's required growth rate (2.15x the frontier prediction) is shown to be a statistical outlier, exceeding even Tesla's record. The essay also scrutinizes the assumptions behind the valuation, including a 79% EBITDA margin and revenue equal to 6% of US GDP. Finally, it argues the IPO structure, with a tiny public float and forced buying by index funds, is designed to create a price that allows insiders to cash out, questioning whether the narrative of a 'Musk frontier' justifies the unprecedented valuation.
