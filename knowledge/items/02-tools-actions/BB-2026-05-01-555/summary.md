## Core Takeaway

Set against the backdrop of a wave of AI company IPOs in the first half of 2026, the article explores why the market is willing to give extremely high valuations to AI companies that are not yet profitable. The author unpacks this from three perspectives: 'scarcity premium,' 'industry substitution potential,' and 'the formula of AI faith.' It begins with Zhipu AI as an example, using limit calculations for three market pools—AI Coding, SaaS substitution, and content production—to show that its valuation has already priced in the rewriting of R&D and software budgets across the entire application layer. It then uses Masayoshi Son's SoftBank as a case study to demonstrate how a vertically integrated path from Arm chips to OpenAI gains market recognition. Finally, it focuses on the lock-up expiration wave for Zhipu AI and MiniMax in July 2026, pointing out that the circulating supply could expand several-fold to tenfold, directly challenging the 'scarcity premium.' The article also compares the 2000 internet bubble with the current AI investment frenzy, concluding that AI companies have real revenue and are backed by tech giants, but their capital expenditure as a percentage of revenue has already surpassed the peak levels of the telecom bubble.
