## Core Takeaway

This VoxEU column addresses the challenge of interpreting conflicting US labour market signals—where payroll growth slows, vacancies decline, but unemployment remains low and wage growth persists. The authors propose a framework that uses principal components analysis on 94 monthly and quarterly time series (1960–2026) and rotates them with sign restrictions from textbook models to produce four 'narrative factors': labour demand, long-run labour supply, short-run labour supply, and matching efficiency. The framework formalizes the narratives policymakers already use implicitly. A key finding is that the post-pandemic soft landing was not solely a demand-side story; a simultaneous contraction in short-run labour supply suppressed the unemployment rate by about 0.75 percentage points and reduced monthly payroll growth by roughly 90,000 jobs. This explains the paradox of households finding it harder to get jobs while firms struggle to fill vacancies. The column emphasizes that no single indicator should be interpreted in isolation and provides a systematic method for organizing noisy, real-time data into coherent economic narratives.
