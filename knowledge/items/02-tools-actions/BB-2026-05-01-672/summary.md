## Core Takeaway

Based on a laboratory study with 58 participants, the authors examine how AI personality personas influence employee performance and well-being. Participants completed a marketing assignment with an AI framed as either a supportive 'servant leader' or a hostile 'dark triad' persona. Using physiological measures (skin conductance, facial electromyography), behavioral analysis, and expert ratings of output quality, the study reveals that hostile AI increases stress (72% higher skin conductance), lengthens conversation cycles, and reduces work quality by roughly one point on a seven-point scale. Critically, self-reported ratings of enjoyment and satisfaction did not reflect these differences, indicating that standard survey tools miss key effects. The article concludes with three practical steps for managers: treat AI persona as a governable design variable, measure friction rather than just adoption, and view employee override attempts as signals of poor AI design rather than misconduct.
