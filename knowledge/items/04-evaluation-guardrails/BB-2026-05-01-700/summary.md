## Core Takeaway

This article examines the growing rift between the Make America Healthy Again (MAHA) movement and the Trump administration. Led by RFK Jr., MAHA supporters—many health-conscious and anti-vaccine—helped elect Trump but are now deeply disappointed by his administration's rollback of vaccine skepticism, pro-pesticide policies, and failure to deliver on health reforms. The piece documents key flashpoints: Trump's executive order boosting glyphosate production, the ouster of Kennedy's allies at HHS, and primary victories by MAHA-backed candidates like Zach Lahn in Iowa. It explores MAHA's evolution from a MAGA ally into an independent political force, and analyzes the difficult path for Democrats who share MAHA's concerns about food and environmental safety but clash over vaccine skepticism. With polling showing a third of Americans identify with MAHA, the movement could become a decisive swing bloc in 2028, though its deep distrust of institutions makes it unpredictable for either party.
