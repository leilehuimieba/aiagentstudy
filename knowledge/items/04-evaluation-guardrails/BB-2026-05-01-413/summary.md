## Core Takeaway

This article, compiled from Anthropic's official blog, introduces a randomized controlled trial on the impact of AI-assisted programming on developer skill development. The experiment recruited 52 software engineers and asked them to complete programming tasks using an unfamiliar Python library, Trio. Results showed that the AI-assisted group scored an average of 50% on the test, while the manual coding group scored 67%, a gap of nearly two grade levels, with the most significant difference in debugging ability. However, not all AI usage patterns led to skill decline: participants who actively asked AI conceptual questions and requested explanations of code logic scored significantly higher than those who relied entirely on AI to generate code. The article categorizes AI interaction patterns into low-scoring modes (full delegation, gradual dependency, iterative debugging) and high-scoring modes (generate-then-understand, code-and-explanation hybrid, conceptual inquiry), and points out that cognitive effort and independent thinking are indispensable for skill mastery. Finally, the article offers suggestions for enterprise managers and individual developers: companies should design mechanisms that promote learning, and individuals should treat AI as a mentor rather than a ghostwriting tool.
