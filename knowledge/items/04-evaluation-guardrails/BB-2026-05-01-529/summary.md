## Core Takeaway

This article from Stratechery analyzes Anthropic's strategic position and actions following the release of its Fable/Mythos models and the subsequent U.S. government export control directive. Ben Thompson argues that while cynics view Anthropic's safety pronouncements as marketing, the company's true strength lies in the perfect alignment between its talent, mission, and business incentives. He explores three imperatives driving Anthropic: the economic imperative to move closer to users and own the touchpoint, the data imperative to collect real-world usage for model improvement, and the power imperative to control the development of frontier AI. Thompson contends that Anthropic's leadership genuinely believes they are the only ones capable of safely developing superintelligence, which justifies a series of self-serving policies—from data retention changes to silently degrading model performance for competitors—all framed as safety measures. He contrasts this with OpenAI's internal conflict and draws a parallel to Apple's user-centric narrative. The article concludes by respecting the effectiveness of this alignment while fearing its implications, as it places immense power in the hands of a group convinced of its own benevolent intentions.
