## Core Takeaway

This essay by Charity Majors, cross-posted from her Substack, responds to the technical side of the debate sparked by her previous piece on AI. She argues that while AI has made code generation cheap and fast (the economics of code production have been inverted), this does not reduce the need for engineering discipline; it increases it. Drawing a powerful analogy to the shift from handcrafted 'pet' servers to immutable 'cattle' infrastructure, she posits that code is becoming a disposable, regenerable artifact—a 'materialized view of understanding.' The real challenge shifts from writing code to defining specifications, validating behavior, and maintaining shared understanding. She champions the role of observability, testing, and production-focused engineering (SRE principles) as the new rigor, arguing that human brains are poor at validation and that AI forces teams to finally adopt the disciplined practices they should have had all along. The essay is a call for engineers to embrace this change by doubling down on engineering fundamentals, not abandoning them.
